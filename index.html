Inside a lesson

In the Quest demo, a learner asks for a ship with solar wings and an ion engine. Here’s how the question becomes a lesson.

Choose where to start

Ask your own question by text or live voice. For a ready-made starting point, browse the School Library and open an exhibit with its original question.

Inspect the exhibit

While a new model is being built, the professor can ask what you expect to see. Once it arrives, turn it around, select a part, and ask how it works.

Choose the next question

Explain what you notice in your own words. The lesson tracks objectives across the conversation, awards XP, and offers follow-up questions based on what you explored.

Professors are AI portrayals inspired by historical figures, not authentic voices or recordings. The project is not affiliated with or endorsed by those people, their estates, or associated institutions. AI explanations and generated exhibits can need correction. Learning outcomes have not been independently established. Exhibit preparation takes time; this is a working prototype.

The world behind the lesson

How an exhibit reaches your room

When a learner requests a new exhibit, our dynamic creation platform sends the design to Blender and brings the finished model into the same lesson.

The professor can respond to a selected part. On a compatible Quest headset, a saved lesson can also restore the exhibit’s position when it recognizes the room.

How this connects to the original vision

The original vision describes historical mentors, Socratic dialogue and immersive learning. In the current prototype:

  • The core world loader, historical mentor engine and guided dialogue are implemented.
  • Quests, XP, levels and achievements exist; unlockable mentors remain future work.
  • The School Library shares starting questions and saved exhibits. Each learner keeps their own progress.
  • Multiplayer classrooms are planned.

Implemented means present in the prototype, with quality and device testing ongoing.
